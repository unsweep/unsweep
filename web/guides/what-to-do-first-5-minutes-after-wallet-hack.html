What to do in the first 5 minutes after a wallet hack

UNSWEEP will never DM you first or ask for your seed phrase or private key. This page is informational; nothing on it is a guarantee of success.

This is a general, informational checklist. It does not guarantee any outcome.

Minute 1: stop the bleeding

  • Do not send more ETH to the compromised address.
  • Do not reply to anyone who DMs you offering recovery: legitimate services never ask for a seed phrase or private key.

Minutes 2–3: create a safe destination

  • Create a new wallet with a new seed phrase, ideally on a device you trust. Do not import the old seed.
  • If the device itself may be infected, use a different device.

Minute 4: assess what is left

  • Check the public address in a block explorer or the read-only free diagnostic.
  • Check for an EIP-7702 delegation (how to check).
  • Note any token approvals to unknown contracts.

Minute 5: decide on a rescue

If tokens are still in the wallet, read why ETH for gas gets stolen and the step-by-step rescue. Funds already taken cannot be recovered; see what can and cannot be rescued.

Scope: Base: 16 verified tokens. Arbitrum: 12 verified tokens. Other ERC-20 tokens may be detected, without guarantee. Ethereum: read-only diagnostic only (no rescue). Funds already taken cannot be recovered and NFTs are not rescued.

FAQ

What should I do first after a wallet hack?

Stop sending ETH to the compromised address and ignore unsolicited recovery offers. Then create a new wallet with a new seed phrase on a trusted device.

Should I share my seed phrase with a recovery service?

No. Never share a seed phrase or private key. UNSWEEP never asks for them and never DMs first.

Can I keep using the hacked wallet?

No. Treat it as permanently compromised and move to a new wallet.

Can stolen funds be recovered?

No. Funds already moved out cannot be recovered; only tokens still in the wallet may be rescued, without guarantee.
