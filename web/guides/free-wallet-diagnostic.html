How to read the free wallet diagnostic

UNSWEEP never writes to you first and never asks for your seed phrase. The only key a rescue uses is the one of the already-compromised wallet, typed by you on unsweep.com and used only inside your browser. This page is informational; nothing on it is a guarantee of success.

Which key, and where it goes: the diagnostic itself never needs a key. A later rescue uses only the private key of the wallet that is already compromised, the one the attacker already has. Never your seed phrase and never the key of your new safe wallet. The key signs 100% inside your browser and is never sent to UNSWEEP or any server; only signatures are sent (see the exact signing code on GitHub). Check it yourself: open DevTools → Network before signing, and see the verified contract on Basescan. UNSWEEP never writes to you first.

What Step 1 · Free Wallet Diagnostic is

Step 1 on the home page is a read-only check of a public address. It is free, you do not sign anything and you do not enter any private key or seed phrase. It reads public on-chain data and summarizes it. It is informational: it does not change anything in the wallet and it is not a guarantee.

What it checks on each network

  • Base mainnet (scan & rescue): native ETH balance, nonce, EIP-7702 delegation state and balances of the tokens in the verified list (16 tokens).
  • Arbitrum mainnet (scan & rescue): the same checks, with the Arbitrum verified list (12 tokens).
  • Ethereum mainnet (read-only check, no rescue): native balance, nonce, EIP-7702 delegation state, main ERC-20 balances and ERC-20 approvals granted by direct approve transactions in the last 90 days.
  • OP Mainnet (chainId 10; free read-only diagnostic, rescue pending): native ETH balance, nonce and EIP-7702 delegation state. Approvals are not checked on OP Mainnet, and rescue on OP Mainnet is not available yet.

Approvals are only reported by the Ethereum read-only check. On Base, Arbitrum and OP Mainnet, do not treat the diagnostic as an approvals audit: check approvals yourself in a block explorer (how to find and revoke ERC-20 approvals).

How to read each block

Wallet

The first lines show the network, the address, the native balance and the nonce. A nonce higher than the number of transactions you remember sending means the key signed transactions you did not make. If the result says Incomplete check, it is NOT a clean result: try again later and do not assume the wallet is safe.

EIP-7702 delegation

If the account code points to a delegate contract, the diagnostic shows its address with a link to the explorer of the selected network (Basescan, Arbiscan, Etherscan or Optimistic Etherscan for OP Mainnet). There are three possible states:

  • Known delegate: the contract is in a short list of known smart-account delegates (MetaMask Smart Account, Coinbase Smart Wallet, Ambire Wallet, Alchemy Modular Account v2 and the UNSWEEP delegate). A known delegate is not a sweeper by itself. If funds keep disappearing, the cause may be a leaked private key or open approvals.
  • Unknown delegate: active delegation to an unrecognized contract. It may be malicious if you did not enable it yourself. Open the address in the explorer and check whether its source is verified and what it does (malicious EIP-7702 delegation). Replacing a delegation does not make a leaked key safe again.
  • No delegation: the account has no EIP-7702 code. This does NOT rule out a classic sweeper bot or a leaked private key (sweeper bot vs delegation).

Tokens

On Base and Arbitrum, the list shows balances of tokens in the verified lists. No rescuable tokens found in our list means none of those tokens were found; other tokens may exist and are not guaranteed to be detected. NFTs may be listed but are not rescued. On Ethereum, the token balances are informational only.

Approvals (Ethereum read-only)

Each line shows a token and the spender that can move it, marked unlimited when applicable. A spender you do not recognize can take that token at any time. The list covers direct approvals from the last 90 days only, so an empty list is not proof that no approvals exist.

What to do with the result

  • Any sign of compromise (unknown delegate, transactions you did not sign, ETH that disappears): treat the wallet as compromised. Create a new wallet on a clean device and stop using the old one. A compromised wallet should be abandoned, not repaired.
  • Do not send ETH “for gas” to a compromised wallet: a bot will take it (why).
  • Tokens in scope on Base or Arbitrum: you can continue to the rescue steps; fees and amounts are shown in a simulation before you sign (step-by-step rescue).
  • Out of scope (Ethereum, NFTs, funds already taken): see what can and cannot be rescued.
  • Clean result: if the key or seed phrase may have been exposed, still treat the wallet as compromised.

FAQ

Does the free diagnostic need my private key or a signature?

No. It only needs the public address. Nothing is signed and no key is entered.

Does 'no delegation' mean my wallet is safe?

No. It does not rule out a classic sweeper bot or a leaked private key. If the key or seed phrase may have been exposed, treat the wallet as compromised and stop using it.

Does the diagnostic list token approvals on Base and Arbitrum?

The approvals list is part of the Ethereum mainnet read-only check. On Base and Arbitrum, do not rely on the diagnostic for approvals; check them in a block explorer.
