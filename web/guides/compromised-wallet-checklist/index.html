Compromised wallet checklist: what to do first

This checklist helps you limit further damage. It cannot undo transfers that already happened, and nobody can honestly promise to recover moved funds. We never ask for any key in chat, email or DM and we never write first. The only key a rescue uses is the one of the already-compromised wallet, typed by you on unsweep.com and used only inside your browser.

1. Assume the key is exposed

  • If funds left without your action, assume the private key or seed phrase is known to someone else. Every account derived from that seed phrase is at risk, on every network.
  • Stop using the wallet for new deposits. Do not reuse the seed phrase anywhere.

2. Do not send gas to the compromised address

Many compromised wallets are watched by a sweeper: a bot or an EIP-7702 delegated contract that forwards any incoming coin within seconds. Sending a small amount to "pay for gas" usually just feeds the attacker.

3. Diagnose with read-only tools

You only need the public address. Nothing should ask you to sign or connect.

  • EIP-7702 delegation: does the account point to a contract you did not choose? See how to check for a sweeper delegation.
  • Nonce: a nonce higher than the number of transactions you sent means someone else is signing.
  • Balances: which tokens remain, including on other networks that share the same address.
  • Open approvals: contracts that can still move your tokens without a new signature. See how to find and revoke approvals.

The free read-only check on this site covers delegation, nonce, balances and recent open approvals on Base and Ethereum mainnet.

4. Set up a clean wallet

  • Create a new wallet with a new seed phrase, preferably on a device you trust or a hardware wallet.
  • Write the seed phrase offline. Never type it into a website, a form or a chat.
  • Move future activity and any assets you still control on other wallets to the new one.

5. Find the cause

  • Recent signatures on unfamiliar sites, "claim" or "airdrop" pages, fake support chats.
  • Downloaded software, browser extensions, cracked apps or files that could include malware.
  • Seed phrase stored in a cloud note, screenshot, email or password manager that may be exposed.
  • If malware is possible, clean or replace the device before creating the new wallet on it.

6. Protect against recovery scams

  • Victims are often targeted a second time by people offering to "recover" funds.
  • Red flags: unsolicited messages, requests for your seed phrase or private key, upfront fees, guaranteed results, pressure to act fast.
  • Legitimate tools let you check an address with the public address alone.

7. Document and report

  • Record the addresses involved, transaction hashes, dates and how you think the compromise happened.
  • Report the theft to local law enforcement. If stolen funds reach a centralized exchange, that exchange's official support channel may be able to act on a police report.

Quick summary

  1. Stop using the wallet and seed phrase.
  2. Do not send gas to it.
  3. Check delegation, nonce, balances and approvals read-only.
  4. Create a fresh wallet on a clean device.
  5. Ignore anyone offering recovery in private messages.

Before you start: trust facts

Sounds familiar? “My funds disappear as soon as they arrive” · “I can’t pay for gas because the ETH is taken instantly” · “My wallet was hacked and I can’t get my tokens out”.

  • Open source: github.com/unsweep/unsweep. Rescue contract 0x38bcF425bC4d13624734e09376F2fafc799fA8c8, source verified on Sourcify (exact match): Base · Arbitrum · OP Mainnet · Basescan.
  • Which key, and where it goes: a rescue uses only the private key of the wallet that is already compromised, the one the attacker already has. Never your seed phrase and never the key of your new safe wallet. The key signs 100% inside your browser and is never sent to UNSWEEP or any server; only signatures are sent (see the exact signing code on GitHub). Check it yourself: open DevTools → Network before signing, and see the verified contract on Basescan. UNSWEEP never writes to you first.
  • We never DM you: UNSWEEP never contacts anyone by DM, Telegram or Discord. Anyone who does is a scammer.
  • Cost: free diagnostic, no upfront payment. Success fee only if the rescue executes, taken from the rescued tokens in the same transaction: 15% (<1,000 USD), 10% (1,000–10,000 USD), 5% (>10,000 USD); hard 15% maximum in the contract; shown in a simulation before you sign.
  • Cannot rescue: funds already taken, NFTs, anything on Ethereum mainnet (read-only). Tokens outside the verified lists may be detected without guarantee. Nothing is guaranteed.
