How to find and revoke ERC-20 token approvals

This guide is informational. Revoking an approval stops future spending; it does not bring back tokens already taken. We never ask for your private key in chat, email or DM, and we will never DM you first.

What an approval is

ERC-20 tokens let an owner authorize another address (the spender, usually a contract) to move up to a set amount of their tokens with transferFrom. The owner grants it with approve(spender, amount). The authorization, called an allowance, stays in force until it is used up or changed. Many apps ask for an "unlimited" amount (2256-1), so one approval can expose the whole balance of that token, now and in the future.

NFT collections (ERC-721 and ERC-1155) have a similar mechanism, setApprovalForAll(operator, true), which gives an operator control over every item of that collection.

Why it matters

  • If a spender contract is malicious or later exploited, it can move approved tokens without asking you again.
  • Phishing pages often ask for an approval instead of a transfer, because it looks harmless.
  • Old approvals to apps you no longer use are unnecessary risk.

How to find open approvals (read-only)

  1. List Approval(owner, spender, value) events where owner is your address, or list your past transactions that call approve (function selector 0x095ea7b3).
  2. For each token and spender pair, call allowance(owner, spender) on the token contract. If the result is greater than 0, the approval is still open.
  3. Check who the spender is in a block explorer: verified source, known app, deployment date.

Example of the read-only call (allowance selector is 0xdd62ed3e):

curl -s -X POST <RPC_URL> -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"eth_call","params":[{"to":"0xTOKEN","data":"0xdd62ed3e<owner padded to 32 bytes><spender padded to 32 bytes>"},"latest"]}'

The free read-only check on this site lists ERC-20 approvals granted by direct transactions in the last 90 days that are still open, on Base and Ethereum mainnet.

Limit: approvals can also be granted by off-chain signatures (permit-style messages) that someone else submits on-chain. Those do not appear in your own transaction history, so also review Approval events for your address if you suspect a malicious signature.

How to revoke

  • From the owner wallet, call approve(spender, 0) on the token contract. This sets the allowance to zero.
  • For NFT collections, call setApprovalForAll(operator, false).
  • Many wallets and block explorers have a built-in approvals page that builds these transactions for you. Use the official site or app, typed by hand, never a link from a message.
  • Each revocation is an on-chain transaction and costs gas.
  • After revoking, call allowance again to confirm it returns 0.

If the wallet is already compromised

  • Revoking needs a signature from the compromised key, and the attacker holds the same key: they can approve again at any time.
  • If a sweeper (a bot or an EIP-7702 delegation) is active, gas you send to pay for the revocation may be taken before you can use it.
  • In that situation, revoking approvals is less important than stopping use of the wallet. See the compromised wallet checklist.

Good habits

  • Approve only the amount a transaction needs when the app allows it.
  • Review approvals every few months and revoke the ones you no longer use.
  • Read what a signature request asks for before signing; treat "approve" and "permit" requests from unknown sites as red flags.
